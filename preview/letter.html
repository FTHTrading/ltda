A letter from the founder

Why I'm still in the game.

I was drafted in the 26th round by the Arizona Diamondbacks. Mike Rizzo, years before he ran the Washington Nationals, came to my grandmother's house and signed me right there. Five dollars and a bus ticket. I danced around that kitchen anyway, because I knew exactly what I'd been handed: a shot.

Baseball has been most of my life ever since. Rick Schu was my hitting coach in the Diamondbacks system, the best I ever had. Later I worked as a player agent at Octagon, hired by Alan Nero, my mentor.

When I ran the baseball program at Montverde Academy, I hired my college coach from Troy, John Mayotte, as head coach for the season. He and his wife moved onto campus. On Scout Day, Cam Walker, my coach at Indian Hills, came too. For one season, both of my college coaches were on the field with me, running the camp.

The older you get, the more clearly you see the game you were part of: what it did right, and who it left behind. Most players never make big-league money. Some of the best people in the game are the coaches, the characters and the creators around it, and the game rarely pays them fairly or keeps a straight record of what they were promised.

That's where this started: making agents and agencies better. Now there's NIL, revenue sharing, and a lot more money moving through the game. So we're building the record for it: the game's history kept where nobody can rewrite it, and players' deals done in the open.

And above all of it, Jesus. None of this happens without Him.

Kevan BurnsFounder · Live the Dream Athletics

Since 2014

Gear

Gloves, bats, balls and cleats for kids who can't buy them. We shipped equipment to young players in Cuba in 2014 and 2015. Next drive: every box logged from the donor to the coach who signs for it.

Since 2001

Coaching

Lessons in Beloit, Wisconsin. The baseball program at Montverde Academy. Clinics at MVP Sport City and Rod Carew Stadium in Panama. Showcases where scouts can see kids who'd otherwise be missed.

Opening

The family fund

Equipment, travel and tournament fees for families who can't cover them. Paid straight to the team or the store, never cash, never a loan. Every grant listed by what it paid for, never by a child's name.
