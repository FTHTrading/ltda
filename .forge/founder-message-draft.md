**Why I'm still in the game**

I was drafted in the 26th round by the Arizona Diamondbacks. Mike Rizzo, years before he ran the Washington Nationals, came to my grandmother's house and signed me right there. Five dollars and a bus ticket. I danced around that kitchen anyway, because I knew exactly what I'd been handed: a shot. [confirm: draft year]

Baseball has been most of my life ever since. Rick Schu was my hitting coach in the Diamondbacks system, the best I ever had. Later I worked as a player agent at Octagon, hired by Alan Nero, my mentor.

When I ran the baseball program at Montverde Academy, I hired my college coach from Troy, John Mayotte, as head coach for the season. He and his wife moved onto campus. On Scout Day, Cam Walker, my coach at Indian Hills, came too. For one season, both of my college coaches were on the field with me, running the camp. [confirm: year; Kevan's title was "director of baseball"]

The older you get, the more clearly you see the game you were part of: what it did right, and who it left behind. Most players never make big-league money. Some of the best people in the game are the coaches, the characters and the creators around it, and the system rarely pays them fairly or keeps a straight record of what they were promised.

That's where this started: making agents and agencies better. Now there's NIL, revenue sharing, and a lot more money moving through the game. So we're building the record for it. An athlete's history, deals and receipts, written down where anyone can check them, and owned by the athlete.

And above all of it, Jesus. None of this happens without Him. [draft wording: Kevan said "you gotta put Jesus as well"; adjust to his own words]

We love the game. We're here to help.
